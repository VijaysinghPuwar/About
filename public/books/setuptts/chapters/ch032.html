Appendix D: Knowledge Check

Try each question before reading its answer.

  1. Why does SetupTTS keep chunks under about 3,600 payload bytes when edge-tts accepts 4,096? So each SetupTTS chunk is exactly one provider request. Above 4,096 bytes edge-tts splits internally, and SetupTTS could no longer retry, verify or range-record the pieces individually. The margin absorbs XML escaping and multi-byte characters.

  2. Why do most English chunks end up around 3,300 characters when the ceiling is 9,200? The payload byte limit binds first. For mostly ASCII text, bytes and characters are nearly equal, so 3,600 bytes means roughly 3,600 characters at most.

  3. A stream ends without any exception. Name the two checks that decide whether its audio is complete. Audio length must reach the end of the last announced sentence (within 0.35 s), and the announced sentences must reach the end of the chunk’s text (within 4 letters).

  4. Why must the resume path use source.txt instead of re-cleaning the user’s text? Cleanup is not idempotent. Re-cleaning can change the text, change its hash, fail the match, and silently restart from chunk 1.

  5. Chunk 3 of 7 is corrupt after a crash, but chunks 4 and 5 look valid. What does resume do with them, and why? It drops 4 and 5 as well and resumes after chunk 2. Trusting only a contiguous prefix keeps the resume position a single number with no gaps to reason about.

  6. What was the root cause of 10-hour texts producing 2 to 3-hour audiobooks, and what replaced it? Irrecoverable recovery fragments returned empty bytes (v1.5.4), so their text was silently absent. v1.5.5 made them raise, recorded per-chunk ranges and sub-ranges, and verified coverage before assembly.

  7. Why does the duration check run before the output file is replaced? So a suspect result can never overwrite an existing good file; it is kept as “(incomplete).mp3” instead.

  8. Why did the fixed 65-second chunk deadline cause failures, and what replaced it? It was shorter than slow voices need to stream a full chunk, so healthy streams were killed. The deadline is now proportional to the expected audio length and speaking rate, and stalls are caught by the short idle timeout.

  9. Which failure kinds skip straight to recovery splitting after two attempts, and why? No-audio failures and timeouts before any audio. Repeating the identical request rarely helps; a smaller request often does.

  10. Why does each TTSWorker create its own asyncio loop? A loop belongs to one thread, and the UI thread runs Qt’s loop. A private loop keeps all network waiting off the UI thread.

  11. How does Cancel reach a coroutine running on another thread safely? loop.call_soon_threadsafe(task.cancel) schedules the cancellation on the worker’s own loop.

  12. What would go wrong if two writers used the same .tmp name when updating the manifest? One writer could rename the other’s half-written file into place. Unique temporary names prevent it.

  13. Why does the Windows release include both an onedir and a onefile build? Onedir starts fast and suits the installer; onefile runs from anywhere, including directly from a zip, which onedir cannot.

  14. What does the minimum-macOS check in CI compare? LSMinimumSystemVersion in the bundle’s Info.plist against the highest minos of every Mach-O binary in the bundle, found with otool -l.

  15. Why will a faster internet plan barely speed up a long job? The data rate is tiny (48 kbit/s of audio); time is spent waiting for the service to synthesise, on per-request latency, and on retries.
