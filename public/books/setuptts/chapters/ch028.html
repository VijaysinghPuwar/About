21 Fifteen Videos to Learn From

Every link below was checked on 7 October 2026 against YouTube’s oEmbed endpoint, which returned the exact title and channel shown. They are ordered as a learning path: Python concurrency first, then the GUI, the network, the audio, reliability, and finally shipping.

1. Python Tutorial: AsyncIO - Complete Guide to Asynchronous Programming with Animations

Channel: Corey Schafer · Difficulty: Beginner to Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oAkLSJNr5zY

What it teaches. Coroutines, tasks, gather, timeouts and the event loop, with animations of what the loop is doing.
Why for SetupTTS. It is the foundation for reading _synthesise_attempt, where every network wait is an await wrapped in asyncio.wait_for.

2. import asyncio: Learn Python’s AsyncIO (playlist)

Channel: EdgeDB · Difficulty: Intermediate to Advanced
Link: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLhNSoGM2ik6SIkVGXWBwerucXjgP1rHmB

What it teaches. A series by CPython core developer Łukasz Langa on how asyncio works inside: the event loop, futures and tasks, and building real programs with it.
Why for SetupTTS. It explains why each TTSWorker needs its own loop, and what call_soon_threadsafe and task cancellation actually do.

3. Learn Python GUI Development for Desktop – PySide6 and Qt Tutorial

Channel: freeCodeCamp.org · Difficulty: Beginner
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z1N9JzNax2k

What it teaches. A full PySide6 course: widgets, layouts, signals and slots, main windows and dialogs.
Why for SetupTTS. It covers the building blocks of MainWindow, the three panels and the dialogs.

4. QtWS17 - Multithreading with Qt - Giuseppe D’Angelo, KDAB

Channel: Qt Group · Difficulty: Intermediate to Advanced
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgqT6SIeRn4

What it teaches. QThread, thread affinity, queued signal connections across threads, and the common mistakes. The examples are C++; the concepts are identical in PySide6.
Why for SetupTTS. This is the theory behind the worker signals, the queued connections into the UI, and the care taken to keep QThread objects alive.

5. The Clean Architecture in Python

Channel: Next Day Video · Difficulty: Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DJtef410XaM

What it teaches. Brandon Rhodes on keeping I/O at the edges and core logic pure and testable.
Why for SetupTTS. It explains why functions such as _take_chunk_at, _stream_incomplete_reason and coverage_report are testable without a network, and suggests how the large worker could be split further.

6. SSL, TLS, HTTPS Explained

Channel: ByteByteGo · Difficulty: Beginner
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9QmMEWmcfo

What it teaches. The TLS handshake, certificates and certificate authorities, in a few minutes.
Why for SetupTTS. It is the background for build_ssl_context and the v1.6.1 fix for antivirus HTTPS scanning.

7. WebSockets Crash Course - Handshake, Use-cases, Pros & Cons and more

Channel: Hussein Nasser · Difficulty: Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Nt-ZrNP22A

What it teaches. The HTTP Upgrade handshake, frames, full-duplex streaming, and what happens when a connection closes.
Why for SetupTTS. Synthesis is a WebSocket session with text and binary frames; this explains how a stream can end without an error.

8. Norman Casagrande: WaveNet, what’s behind Google’s voice

Channel: Electromagnetic Field · Difficulty: Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gIIZyYsB81M

What it teaches. How text-to-speech moved from stitched recordings to neural models that generate audio sample by sample.
Why for SetupTTS. It explains what the remote service is doing during the seconds SetupTTS spends waiting, and why some voices stream slower than others. It is about Google’s system, not Microsoft’s, but the principles are shared.

9. Digital Audio Compression - Computerphile

Channel: Computerphile · Difficulty: Beginner
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KGZ0een8vSE

What it teaches. How lossy audio compression such as MP3 works.
Why for SetupTTS. It is the background for the 48 kbit/s mono format, and for why frames can be concatenated and counted in mp3_duration.py.

10. Thundering Herd Problem and How not to do API retries

Channel: Arpit Bhayani · Difficulty: Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8sTuCPh3s0s

What it teaches. Why naive retries make outages worse, and how exponential back-off and jitter help.
Why for SetupTTS. It lets you judge _process_chunk’s retry policy, including why its lack of jitter is acceptable at two clients and would not be at two thousand.

11. Distributed Systems lecture series (playlist)

Channel: Martin Kleppmann · Difficulty: Intermediate to Advanced
Link: https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLeKd45zvjcDFUEv_ohr_HdUFe97RItdiB

What it teaches. Kleppmann’s Cambridge course: system models, failure detection, fault tolerance and the impossibility of certainty over an unreliable network.
Why for SetupTTS. It gives the theory behind timeouts as failure detectors, and behind the decision to verify a stream’s completeness instead of trusting the absence of an error.

12. atomic file replacement (os.rename vs os.replace) (intermediate) anthony explains #264

Channel: anthonywritescode · Difficulty: Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-9eXCb3yvyY

What it teaches. Writing to a temporary file and atomically replacing the target, and the cross-platform differences between os.rename and os.replace.
Why for SetupTTS. It is exactly the pattern of _atomic_write_bytes, AppSettings.save and the final output replacement.

13. Dustin Ingram - Inside the Cheeseshop: How Python Packaging Works - PyCon 2018

Channel: PyCon 2018 · Difficulty: Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AQsZsgJ30AE

What it teaches. The history and mechanics of Python packaging: sdists, wheels, setuptools and pyproject.toml.
Why for SetupTTS. It explains pyproject.toml, requirements.txt pins, and what PyInstaller is collecting from site-packages.

14. Packaging with PyInstaller

Channel: Michigan Python · Difficulty: Intermediate
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S_Bus_FNjpg

What it teaches. How PyInstaller bundles an application: spec files, onefile versus onedir, hidden imports and data files.
Why for SetupTTS. It maps directly onto setuptts.spec, setuptts_portable.spec, and the onefile/onedir history in Chapter 15. It is a community meetup talk; no larger channel covers PyInstaller in comparable depth.

15. GitHub Actions Tutorial - Basic Concepts and CI/CD Pipeline with Docker

Channel: TechWorld with Nana · Difficulty: Beginner
Link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R8_veQiYBjI

What it teaches. Workflows, events, jobs, runners and a complete CI/CD pipeline. The deployment part uses Docker, which SetupTTS does not, but the workflow concepts are the same.
Why for SetupTTS. It is the vocabulary needed to read and extend .github/workflows/build.yml.
