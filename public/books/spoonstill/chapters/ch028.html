23.1 Where the time goes

All of these are the project's own measurements on named machines (macOS arm64 with FFmpeg 8.0.1 for §1 to §10; Windows 11 with an RTX 3060 for §13). This book did not re-measure them.

23.2 Parallelism

The default worker count is half the cores, at most 4. On macOS (12 scenes at 1080p) the speed-up was 1.55x at 2 workers, 1.83x at 3, 1.85x at 4, 2.02x at 6, and 1.92x at 8: it flattens because x264 already uses about 2.7 cores per worker (§10). On a 16-core Windows machine, 4 workers took 19.9 s and 12 took 19.8 s (§13b). More workers mostly buy memory use.

23.3 Memory

Each worker's peak memory follows the prescale canvas. Measured per FFmpeg child (§12): 369 MB at 720p, 768 MB at 1080p, 1 219 MB at 1440p, 2 630 MB at 4K. The capacity model (192 MiB + 36 bytes per prescale pixel) sits 5% to 29% above every measurement on purpose. With a budget of 70% of RAM, an 8 GB machine gets 2 workers at 4K instead of the 4 that froze a real machine (D-144).

23.4 Caching is the biggest lever

A second render reuses everything: in the analysis run, the 6-scene fixture's first render took 1.4 s and the second re-encoded nothing. At the design point, the project measured 500 scenes at 1080p: cold renders of 161, 151 and 154 s; 499 of 500 segments reused after editing one narration; and a resume after a kill reusing exactly the 167 finished scenes (D-154).

Single-flight caching avoids duplicate work: eight workers needing one narration do it once (D-108); one photo used by many scenes is hashed once (D-173); one silence file is measured once per run instead of 500 times (D-184).

23.5 Overlapping network and CPU

Before D-146, all narrations were resolved first and then all segments rendered. With speech coming from the network, the CPU sat idle during fetching and vice versa. The pipeline overlaps them; with a stand-in voice service that waits before answering, 12 scenes went from about 22 to 25 s down to about 14 to 16 s, three runs out of three, with no extra memory.

23.6 Scaling with scene count

Stage Cost as scenes grow
validation linear in scenes, parallel probes (two per scene)
audio linear in distinct narrations; zero for cached
segments linear in distinct segment keys; zero for cached
join linear in bytes (stream copy), one process
film check one header probe, independent of scene count
chapter cutting linear in words (a test asserts a 4x input takes far less than 16x the time)

At 431 real scenes the author's cold render took 8 min 50 s with no service refusals; a 40-fold stress test (17 240 scenes, a 20 h 45 min film) completed (D-195, Not independently verified).

23.7 Things that hurt performance

  • Undersized photos. Rendering 1376x768 stills into a 4K frame makes FFmpeg work on an 11520x6480 canvas for no extra detail. spoonstill warns and suggests a smaller --short-edge (D-145). This is often the biggest real-world lever.
  • Network volumes. Joining onto a network share took 38.9 s against 6.4 s locally in one log, and caused probe stalls and damaged segments (findings.md F-18, D-178, D-179).
  • The TTS service throttle. The pace gate limits starts to 50 per minute, so a cold project of 500 spoken lines needs at least ten minutes of speech requests.
