3 Video and Audio From Zero

This chapter teaches just enough multimedia to read the rest of the book. Each idea is tied to the place spoonstill uses it.

3.1 Frames and frame rate

A video is a sequence of still pictures called frames, shown quickly enough that motion looks smooth. Frame rate (fps) is how many frames per second. spoonstill's default is 30 fps and it accepts 1 to 120 (OutputSpec::new in crates/spoonstill-core/src/geometry.rs).

spoonstill connection. A scene's length is a whole number of frames. The rule (frames_for_duration, crates/spoonstill-core/src/timing.rs:25-38) is: frames = round up (narration seconds x fps). A 3.717 s narration at 30 fps is 111.51, rounded up to 112 frames, which is 3.7333 s. The extra 16 ms is filled with silence. Rounding down would cut off the last syllable.

3.2 Resolution and aspect

Resolution is the frame's size in pixels, for example 1920x1080. Aspect ratio is its shape: 16:9 landscape, 9:16 portrait (Shorts, Reels, TikTok), 1:1 square.

spoonstill connection. You name the short edge, not the width: 1080p means 1920x1080 in landscape and 1080x1920 in portrait. The four named sizes are 720p, 1080p, 1440p (also called 2k) and 2160p (4k). 4K is the ceiling; 8K is refused (Chapter 15 explains why it is derived from the H.264 level table).

16:9 9:16 1:1
720p 1280x720 720x1280 720x720
1080p (default) 1920x1080 1080x1920 1080x1080
1440p / 2k 2560x1440 1440x2560 1440x1440
2160p / 4k 3840x2160 2160x3840 2160x2160

3.3 Pixels, pixel formats and colour range

Each pixel stores colour. Video rarely stores red, green and blue directly; it stores YUV: brightness (Y) plus two colour-difference channels (U, V). yuv420p means the colour channels are stored at a quarter of the resolution (half width, half height), which the eye barely notices and which saves space. It also means the frame's width and height must be even numbers, which is why OutputSpec::new refuses odd sizes.

Colour range is subtle. "Limited" or tv range uses brightness values 16 to 235; "full" or pc range uses 0 to 255. JPEG photos are full range. If that flag leaks through, FFmpeg labels the output yuvj420p, a different format.

spoonstill connection. The project measured this trap (ffmpeg-findings.md §7b) and pins colour explicitly in the filter chain: out_range=tv on the first scale and setparams=range=tv:color_primaries=bt709:color_trc=bt709:colorspace=bt709 near the end (decision D-037). Every segment must come out yuv420p, tv, bt709, or it fails the profile check.

3.4 Sample aspect ratio (SAR)

Some files say their pixels are not square. SAR (sample aspect ratio) records that. A 1999x1001 photo, scaled and cropped, can come out of FFmpeg with SAR 30007:30000: nearly square, but not exactly, and players will stretch the picture slightly.

spoonstill connection. setsar=1 is always the last filter before format=yuv420p (D-033), and the test fixture odd.jpg is generated at exactly 1999x1001 and asserted to be odd so the test can never pass by accident (scripts/gen-fixtures.sh).

3.5 Codecs and containers

A codec is a way to compress video or audio. H.264 (also called AVC) is the most widely supported video codec; AAC is the matching audio codec. A container is the file format that holds compressed streams together with timing information: MP4 is the container here.

  • Encoding turns raw frames into compressed data. spoonstill uses libx264, a software H.264 encoder, by default.
  • Muxing (multiplexing) puts the video and audio streams into the container. Demuxing takes them back out.
  • Profile and level are H.264 labels saying which features and how much work a decoder needs. spoonstill always asks for the High profile and computes the level from the frame size and rate (h264_level in crates/spoonstill-media/src/profile.rs:381-412): 1080p30 is level 4.0, 4K30 is level 5.1.

3.6 Quality settings: CRF and presets

libx264 has a CRF (constant rate factor) setting: lower means better quality and bigger files; 18 is visually near-lossless for most material. The preset trades encoding speed for compression efficiency; medium is the balanced default. Both are configurable in project.yaml under encode:.

3.7 Key frames, and why concatenation is easy here

H.264 stores some frames completely (key frames, or I-frames) and most others as differences from nearby frames. Each spoonstill segment is an independent file that starts with a key frame, so segments can be joined end to end with a stream copy: the compressed bytes are copied, nothing is decoded or re-encoded. That is fast, but it requires every segment to have identical parameters. FFmpeg does not check this for you (Chapter 16).

3.8 Time bases

Inside a container, each frame has a timestamp counted in ticks of a time base. spoonstill pins the video time base to 1/90000 (-video_track_timescale 90000), because 90 000 divides evenly by 24, 25, 30, 50 and 60 fps. Without the pin, the MP4 muxer chooses a time base per frame rate (1/15360 at 30 fps), and two segments with different time bases would join badly.

3.9 Digital audio

Sound is a pressure wave. Digital audio measures it many times a second: the sample rate. spoonstill normalizes every narration to 48 000 samples per second, stereo (2 channels), 16-bit PCM (pcm_s16le) in a WAV file. PCM means uncompressed. Using uncompressed audio for the intermediate files avoids compressing twice (once in the source MP3, once in the final AAC).

Bitrate is how many bits per second a compressed stream uses. The final AAC audio uses 192 kb/s.

spoonstill connection. Because the audio is cut by sample count, the trim is exact: a 112-frame scene at 30 fps needs exactly 112 x 48000 / 30 = 179 200 samples, and the filter says atrim=end_sample=179200, not a rounded decimal time.

3.10 Loudness

Different recordings are recorded at different volumes. LUFS (Loudness Units relative to Full Scale) measures perceived loudness. spoonstill measures each narration and applies one constant gain so it lands at -16 LUFS integrated, without letting the peak exceed -1.5 dBTP (true peak), and never boosting more than 24 dB (Verified from source: crates/spoonstill-media/src/audio.rs:72-84). The constant gain is deterministic, which matters for caching (Chapter 17).

3.11 Filters and filter graphs

A filter transforms frames or audio samples: scale, crop, pad, overlay, resample. FFmpeg chains filters with commas and connects chains with labelled pads into a filter graph:

[0:v]scale=...,crop=...,zoompan=...,setparams=...,setsar=1,format=yuv420p[v];
[1:a]aresample=48000,apad,atrim=end_sample=179200,asetpts=N/SR/TB[a]

[0:v] means "the video of input 0", [v] names the result. -filter_complex passes the whole graph as one argument.

3.12 The Ken Burns effect

In plain words: a still photo is larger than the visible frame; a window slowly changes position or size; repeated frames make the photo appear to move. It is named after the documentary filmmaker who popularised it.

spoonstill's version, in one sentence: scale the photo up to three times the output size so it covers the frame, crop it to the output's shape, then let FFmpeg's zoompan filter move a window over it, producing exactly N output frames from one still. Chapter 15 works through the arithmetic with real numbers.

3.13 Putting it together for one scene

                     |  filter graph: cover-fit 3x, crop, zoompan (N frames),
                     |  pin colour, setsar=1, yuv420p
                     v
                H.264 video, N frames ------+
                                            +--> mux into seg-<key>.mp4
normalized WAV --> pad + trim to exact      |    (checked with ffprobe)
                   sample count --> AAC ----+
